A new robotic disassembly system can unscrew, manipulate, and mill through defective devices while preserving valuable parts from damage, according to IEEE Spectrum. The work aims to advance the automated circular economy by enabling the recovery of reusable components from end-of-life electronics.

The system is designed to handle the variability of discarded products, which often arrive in unpredictable conditions. By combining multiple manipulation and material-removal capabilities, the robot can adapt to different device geometries and extract components without destroying them. This capability is critical for making recycling economically viable, as intact parts retain higher value than shredded materials.

> This robotic disassembling system is capable of unscrewing, manipulating, and milling through defective devices while **preserving valuable parts from damage**.

While the article does not provide specific performance metrics or experimental details, the concept addresses a key bottleneck in e-waste processing: the labor-intensive and hazardous task of manual disassembly. Automating this process could reduce costs and improve safety, while also supporting sustainability goals by keeping components in circulation longer.

The development signals a step toward practical robotic recycling, though further details on the system's capabilities and limitations are not disclosed in the source.
