Agile Robots has closed a $500 million Series F funding round, bringing its valuation to $8.5 billion and total capital raised to $2 billion. The company builds humanoids, mobile robots, robotic arms, and robotic hands, along with the software and AI models that drive them — a full-stack physical AI play aimed squarely at industrial deployment.

SoftBank Group led the round, with participation from NVIDIA, Hitachi Group (Hitachi and Hitachi Ventures), and T.Capital, alongside other investors spanning automotive, AI, digital, and industrial technology. The investor mix signals confidence that physical AI is moving from lab demonstration to production-floor reality.

> The company builds humanoids, mobile robots, robotic arms, and robotic hands, along with the software and AI models that drive them — **a full-stack physical AI play aimed squarely at industrial deployment.**

Agile Robots' customer base spans automotive, consumer electronics, healthcare, and service industries. CEO and Founder Dr. Zhaopeng Chen framed the raise as a mandate to industrialize: "Physical AI will fundamentally change how the world builds, moves and makes things. Our vision is intelligent robots working alongside people in factories and warehouses everywhere, and Agile Robots is determined to lead that shift. We bring physical AI out of the lab and into industrial production, across logistics, automotive, electronics and manufacturing. This funding allows us to accelerate our global expansion, strengthen our presence in key markets and build out production capacity."

SoftBank's Dennis Chang, managing partner at SoftBank Investment Advisers, said the firm believes physical AI will transform industries and that Agile Robots "combines deep robotics expertise with the ability to execute at industrial scale." He added that SoftBank is pleased to continue partnering with Dr. Chen and the team as they advance industrial-scale automation.

The company said the funding will support increased production capacity and expansion into key markets, with plans to strengthen its presence in the United States and accelerate development efforts. The capital injection positions Agile Robots to scale manufacturing output as demand for physical AI systems grows across logistics, automotive, electronics, and general manufacturing.
