Researchers at the Technical University of Munich (TUM) have developed a soft, lightweight exosuit that assists hip extension using artificial tendons, helping older adults rise from a chair and walk more efficiently. The work, from Professor Lorenzo Masia's lab, targets a movement most people take for granted but which becomes difficult as hip and leg strength and coordination decline.

The suit is designed for a population that includes elderly people and those with medical conditions, and the same technology is also aimed at industrial workers performing heavy tasks. The central challenge the team addresses is timing: because standing involves a precise sequence of coordinated leg and hip motion, a robot-assisted suit must respond intuitively and synchronously to the wearer's body.

> "We use machine learning to provide the user with the necessary assistance at exactly the right moment," says researcher Dr. Xiaohui Zhang from the Chair of Intelligent Bio-Robotic Systems of the TUM School of Computation, Information and Technology.

To build the recognition system and train the algorithm, the researchers first collected movement data from a group of young, healthy individuals. The system is designed to be put on and taken off like a suit in just a few minutes, and comprises two thigh straps, a waist belt, and artificial tendons. The team notes that as more usage data becomes available, the system can be further refined.

The source does not report quantitative results, such as measured reductions in muscle effort, timing accuracy, or participant outcomes. It also does not specify the suit's weight, actuation hardware, control latency, or the size and composition of the training dataset. The researchers indicate that continued data collection will allow further refinement of the assistance system.
