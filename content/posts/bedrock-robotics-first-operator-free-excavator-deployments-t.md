Bedrock Robotics Inc. has deployed fully autonomous, retrofitted heavy excavators onto active infrastructure projects in Texas and Nevada, marking a shift toward software-driven earthwork in the construction industry. The company says these AI-driven heavy equipment projects directly address the sector's severe labor shortages, safety hazards, and cost overruns.

As the U.S. faces a looming labor crisis—with over 40% of the construction workforce expected to retire within five years—Bedrock said its technology comes at a pivotal moment for national infrastructure ambitions. Backed by $350 million in venture capital and led by former Waymo autonomy veterans, the startup's plug-and-play retrofit system turns complex, high-risk earthwork into software-driven operations.

> The San Francisco-based company asserted that it is helping the physical economy transition from digital AI concepts to **self-orchestrating machine fleets on live job sites**.

The first few projects for Bedrock Robotics include a water treatment facility in partnership with Sundt Construction in Nevada, a multi-million cubic yard earthwork site with Champion Site Prep in Texas, and a 1.2 million cubic yard civil sitework project with Zachry Construction Corp. Bedrock claimed that these deployments mark a significant shift, as AI increasingly performs work of genuine economic value in the physical world.

According to Kevin Peterson, chief technology officer of Bedrock Robotics, the current scope involves clearing earth, early earthwork, cut and fill operations, prep work for foundations, and rough cuts ahead of more detailed work. The company sees this as a critical step toward fleets that can self-orchestrate across machine types to plan, share, and execute jobs on a construction site at a pace and scale the industry has never seen.
