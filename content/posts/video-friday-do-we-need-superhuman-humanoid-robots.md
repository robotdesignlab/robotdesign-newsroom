This week's Video Friday roundup from IEEE Spectrum showcases a diverse array of humanoid robots performing tasks that range from drumming and welding to farming, playing soccer, helping at home, and operating heavy equipment. The featured videos highlight the expanding capabilities of humanoid robots beyond traditional industrial applications, raising questions about the necessity and desirability of superhuman performance.

As humanoid robots become more versatile, the question of whether they should exceed human physical capabilities becomes increasingly relevant. The videos demonstrate that while some tasks require human-like dexterity and adaptability, others might benefit from enhanced strength, speed, or endurance. This prompts a broader discussion about the design philosophy behind humanoid robots and the balance between mimicking human form and surpassing human limitations.

> Most humans can’t do this, but then, **why would we want to?**

The roundup, curated by IEEE Spectrum's robotics editor Evan Ackerman, reflects the growing trend of humanoids entering diverse sectors, from domestic assistance to specialized industrial operations. The variety of tasks shown suggests that the future of humanoid robotics may not be about replicating human abilities exactly, but about augmenting them in ways that are useful and safe for human-robot collaboration.

While the article does not provide specific technical specifications or deployment timelines, it underscores the rapid progress in humanoid robotics and the importance of considering the societal and ethical implications of creating robots that can outperform humans in certain domains. As the field advances, the conversation about what we truly need from humanoid robots will likely intensify.
