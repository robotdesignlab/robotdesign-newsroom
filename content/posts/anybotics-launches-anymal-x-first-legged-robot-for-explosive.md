ANYbotics AG has launched ANYmal X, which it claims is the world's first legged inspection robot certified for explosive atmospheres. The quadruped is designed to meet strict safety standards in oil, gas, and chemical plants, where standard robots are banned and human operators still require personal protective equipment.

The Zurich-based company, spun out of ETH Zurich in 2016, says its ANYmal robots and Shift operations platform automate routine inspections across multiple industries, keeping people out of harm's way and turning inspection data into maintenance action. ANYmal X uses an advanced industrial AI stack to conduct autonomous inspections in environments that are dangerous to people.

> "Many experts thought a legged robot could never be made safe for explosive atmospheres."

ANYmal X is the product of four years of engineering and more than 10 patents, according to CEO Péter Fankhauser. "Oil, gas and chemical plants are among the most hazardous and most costly places to operate," he said, adding that these areas were previously off-limits to robots like ANYbotics'.

The company values the global oil, gas, and chemical sector at $102 billion, describing it as the largest market for autonomous inspection. Two major operational costs drive this market: the expensive skilled labor required for manual inspection and the downtime caused by preventable equipment failures. Critical components such as pumps, compressors, valves, and piping are located in hazardous zones, creating a prime use case for legged robots that can operate where standard machines cannot.

ANYbotics is backed by more than $150 million from European and Silicon Valley investors and has about 200 employees across Zurich, San Francisco, and Barcelona. The launch marks the beginning of deliveries for ANYmal X, though the source does not specify deployment timelines or target customers.
