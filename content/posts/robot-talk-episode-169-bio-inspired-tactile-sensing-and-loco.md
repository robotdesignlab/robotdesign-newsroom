Robot Talk's latest episode features a conversation with Dexter Shepherd of the University of Sussex on applying artificial intelligence to help robots understand and adapt to their environments. The discussion centers on machine learning for bio-inspired tactile sensing and robotic locomotion — two capabilities that let machines interpret physical contact and adjust how they move.

Shepherd is a Postdoctoral Researcher at the University of Sussex, where his work spans machine learning for bio-inspired tactile sensing and robotic locomotion. According to the episode description, his research combines embedded AI systems, spatial-temporal models, and reinforcement learning, with the aim of enabling robots to classify textures, orient themselves, and adapt their movements.

The approach brings together three technical threads: embedded AI systems that run computation on-device, spatial-temporal models suited to processing contact signals over time, and reinforcement learning for adaptive control.
