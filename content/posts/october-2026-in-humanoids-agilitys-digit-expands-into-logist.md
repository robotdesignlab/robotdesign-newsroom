October 2026 brought a cluster of humanoid robot developments, with Agility Robotics' Digit humanoid at the center of two commercial announcements and Boston Dynamics revealing new detail on its redesigned humanoid hand. The month's most-read coverage on The Robot Report points to a sector shifting from viral demonstrations toward factory and warehouse deployment agreements.

On the commercial side, AGCO and Agility Robotics signed an agreement in October 2026 to deploy the Digit humanoid in AGCO's manufacturing operations. The U.S. pilot is expected to begin in 2027 and will start at AGCO's site in Hesston, Kansas, supporting the company's push toward more flexible, intelligent factories. Separately, Agility Robotics and GXO Logistics said they are expanding their partnership to deploy Digit at GXO sites in North Carolina and Tennessee.

Boston Dynamics used the month to give more insight into its redesigned humanoid hand. The four-fingered gripper has 13 degrees of freedom and is directly actuated, and the company said it designed the hand for mass production.

The month's coverage also included a broader argument about robot form factor: that robots should follow workflow rather than chase spectacle. The piece noted that viral humanoid feats — balancing on one leg, backflips, folding laundry, and traversing difficult terrain — matter because they demonstrate embodied AI has reached a new technical stage, while asking whether less flashy robots will win in real-world settings.

Beyond the humanoid items, October 2026 marked the 20th anniversary of RoboBusiness, with the announcement that next year the show will be rebranded Robotics Summit & Expo West. Promise Robotics also partnered with D.R. Horton on physical AI for homebuilding.

The through-line for humanoids is deployment timing: Agility's AGCO pilot is slated for 2027, while its GXO expansion targets logistics sites in two additional states. Boston Dynamics' emphasis on designing its hand for mass production signals the same directional shift — from capability demonstrations toward manufacturable, deployable hardware.
