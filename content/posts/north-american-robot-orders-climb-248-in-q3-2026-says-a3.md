North American companies ordered 10,989 robots valued at $723.5 million in the third quarter of 2026, according to the Association for Advancing Automation (A3). The figures mark a 24.8% increase in units and a 26.1% increase in value over the third quarter of 2025, making Q3 the strongest quarter of the year so far.

The gains came as automotive OEM orders, which had declined in the first half of 2026, returned to positive year-over-year growth. A3, based in Ann Arbor, Michigan, reported that the third-quarter performance lifted total orders for the first nine months of 2026 to 28,984 robots valued at approximately $1.89 billion — increases of 9.6% in units and 13.3% in value over the same period last year.

> Orders increased 24.8% in units and 26.1% in value compared with the third quarter of 2025, **making it the strongest quarter of the year so far**.

Automotive OEM orders rose 7.5% in Q3, while orders from automotive component suppliers climbed 90.4%, continuing strong demand from that segment seen earlier in the year. Several other industries posted substantial unit increases: semiconductors and electronics/photonics rose 97.6%, plastics and rubber 67.7%, life sciences/pharmaceutical/biomedical 38.7%, and metals 37.0%. All other industries increased just 0.5% in units, while food and consumer goods declined 23.7%.

A3 attributed the electronics growth to expanding investment in U.S. semiconductor manufacturing, with strong demand for electronics supporting AI and other applications. It also pointed to continued investment in domestic pharmaceutical manufacturing as a driver in life sciences. Despite the Q3 improvement, automotive OEM orders remained 17% below their year-earlier level through the first nine months of 2026.
