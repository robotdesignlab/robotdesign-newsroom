Medtronic has received U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) clearance for its Hugo robotic-assisted surgery system to be used in general surgery procedures, expanding the modular, cart-based platform beyond its existing urology indication. The approval allows Hugo to perform surgeries across the abdomen, a significant expansion for the system in the U.S. market.

The clearance covers all upper abdominal general surgical procedures, including cholecystectomy, Roux-en-Y gastric bypass, ventral hernia, and small bowel resection. The FDA also cleared lower abdominal procedures of inguinal hernia and appendectomy. Medtronic said these procedures combined account for about 3.5 million surgeries annually in the U.S. The company already has experience in many of these general surgery areas, noting its technologies in open and laparoscopic approaches have been used for more than fifty years.

Medtronic received CE marking for general surgery in Europe in 2022, which provided clinical data to support its U.S. submission. "We started our U.S. journey in February of last year," Rajit Kamal, general manager of robotic surgical technologies at Medtronic, told The Robot Report. "We have been pleased with the progress we have made. We are in many leading hospitals in the country with our urology indication. Our general surgery further opens those opportunities for us and for our combined value proposition, not just for Hugo, but in service, training, support, touch surgery, and the broader portfolio." Globally, Hugo is on track to surpass 50,000 cumulative procedures by the end of the Medtronic fiscal year. "General surgery has been available on Hugo outside of the U.S., and it is our fastest-growing procedural mix, because we, as Medtronic, have a strong value proposition in general surgery," Kamal said. "Many of [the surgeries] are multi-quad" (the quote was truncated in the source).
