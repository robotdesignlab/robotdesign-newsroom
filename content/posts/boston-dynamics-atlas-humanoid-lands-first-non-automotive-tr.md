GlobalFoundries will become the first customer outside the automotive industry to test Boston Dynamics' Atlas humanoid, with testing set to begin in early 2027. The semiconductor manufacturer is expanding an existing relationship with Boston Dynamics that already includes deployments of the Spot quadruped for industrial inspections.

The agreement gives Boston Dynamics an opportunity to validate Atlas in a manufacturing environment outside that of its parent company, Hyundai Motor Group. While automotive manufacturing has emerged as an early proving ground for humanoids, semiconductor manufacturing could present a different set of technical and operational requirements.

> The agreement gives Boston Dynamics an opportunity to validate Atlas in a manufacturing environment **outside that of its parent company, Hyundai Motor Group.**

GlobalFoundries has already been a Boston Dynamics customer, having deployed Spot for industrial inspections for several years. Since April 2026, two Spot robots at its Singapore facility have collectively traveled nearly 900 km (559 mi.) and completed more than 90,000 inspections in sub-fab areas, achieving a 99% mission success rate, according to the companies. The companies are also expanding that relationship to include additional Spot deployments across GlobalFoundries' global manufacturing network.

Under the new effort, Boston Dynamics and GlobalFoundries will identify potential humanoid applications in semiconductor manufacturing, assess integration requirements and customer readiness, and begin training Atlas to perform specific tasks. The companies did not disclose how many Atlas robots will be involved, which facilities will host the humanoids, or what initial tasks they will perform.

"Spot has helped us shift from reactive to predictive maintenance, collecting data across thousands of inspection points to prevent downtime and keep our fabs running more efficiently," said Pradip Singh of GlobalFoundries.

The early-2027 Atlas testing marks a key step in Boston Dynamics' efforts to commercialize its humanoid, extending its customer base beyond automotive manufacturing.
