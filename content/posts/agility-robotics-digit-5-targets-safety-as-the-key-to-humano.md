Agility Robotics has unveiled Digit 5, a humanoid robot that IEEE Spectrum describes as potentially the first humanoid robot worker that is truly safe. The company says the new machine is big enough, and safe enough, to do useful work, positioning safety as the central requirement for putting humanoids into real industrial settings.

Agility does not yet have Digit 5 deployed in factories, but the hardware is real, as photographs accompanying the coverage show. That distinction matters for a sector where many humanoid announcements remain concepts or pilot programs rather than physical systems capable of performing useful labor.

> Agility does not yet have Digit 5 deployed in factories, but **the hardware is real**, as photographs accompanying the coverage show.

The available reporting does not specify Digit 5's height, weight, payload capacity, walking speed, degrees of freedom, battery life, sensor suite, or actuator design. What the source does establish is the framing: the robot is characterized as large enough and safe enough to be useful, and safety is presented as the attribute that would make it the first humanoid worker to genuinely meet that bar. No pricing, availability date, or certification details are provided.

The source does not discuss deployment timelines, target markets, or commercial implications beyond noting that factory deployment has not yet occurred. Readers should treat the safety and usefulness claims as Agility Robotics' positioning rather than independently verified performance data.
