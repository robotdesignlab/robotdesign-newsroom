Global demand for autonomous mobile robots (AMRs) is again on the rise, propelled by labor costs and shortages, as well as the need for greater operational flexibility and speed. At the same time, the technology continues to evolve, as AMRs become smarter and more nimble.

According to a new report from The Robot Report and Design World, the global market for autonomous mobile robots will grow from nearly $5 billion in 2024 to $14 billion in 2030, as predicted by Interact Analysis. While the market slowed immediately after the COVID-19 pandemic, it shows signs of a strong recovery. Similarly, ABI Research has forecast that the mobile robot market could expand from $33 billion in 2026 to $68.9 billion by 2030, at a compound annual growth rate (CAGR) of 20.2%, with AMRs accounting for half of that total.

> The global market for autonomous mobile robots will grow from nearly $5 billion in 2024 to **$14 billion in 2030**.

Much of the recent capability improvements are thanks to computer vision, sensor fusion, and machine learning. Not only do they enable efficient navigation, but they also allow for precise picking, safer collaboration, and improved visibility. In addition, fleet management and artificial intelligence are making fleets of these systems easier to scale and integrate with existing enterprise systems and software.

Automated and semi-automated forklifts can be easily integrated into existing warehouse and factory workflows, say suppliers. For those more willing to invest in infrastructure, the shuttles in automated storage and retrieval systems (ASRS) offer dense space utilization and scalability. Interoperability challenges remain, but orchestration software vendors and standards developers have risen to the challenge.

From autonomous lift trucks from Hyster-Yale to ASRS from Attabotics, the latest generation of mobile robots is also moving into manufacturing, pharmaceuticals, and other sectors. The report highlights these advances as AMRs continue to expand their role in industrial automation.
