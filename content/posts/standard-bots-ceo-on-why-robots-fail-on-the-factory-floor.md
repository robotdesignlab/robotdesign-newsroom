In Episode 270 of The Robot Report Podcast, host Evan Beard — co-founder, CEO, and chief engineer of Standard Bots — discusses the hidden reason most robots fail on the factory floor. Beard's company is described as one of America's largest manufacturers of AI-native industrial robots, and the conversation centers on building the full robotics stack in-house rather than assembling it from outside parts.

Standard Bots develops its entire robotics stack internally, spanning hardware, software, and AI. The company designs nearly all of its own components, including actuators, and assembles every robot at its facility in Glen Cove, New York. That vertical integration is framed as central to the company's approach to reliability in industrial settings.

> Standard Bots develops its entire robotics stack internally, spanning hardware, software, and AI, and **designs nearly all of its own components, including actuators**.

The company's long-term vision extends beyond assembly: it aims to build a fully domestic manufacturing operation, from raw metal to finished robots. Beard founded Standard Bots on the premise that the country which builds the robots of the future will build everything in the future. He has testified before Congress twice, advocating for a coordinated U.S. robotics strategy and a national approach to advancing the industry. Beard graduated with honors from Duke University, where he studied economics and computer science.

The episode's news segment also touched on broader sector developments, including a Silicon Valley Leadership Group publication on Physical AI in Controlled Environments, NVIDIA replacing its CEO amid China AI and robotics concerns, Berkeley research on whether AI agents can program industrial robots without training data, and Boston Dynamics adding wheels to its Atlas humanoid to test whole-body control.
